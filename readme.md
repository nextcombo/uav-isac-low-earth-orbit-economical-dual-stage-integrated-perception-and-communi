# UAV+ISAC: Low-Earth Orbit Economical Dual-Stage Integrated Perception and Communication (ISAC) Framework Based on 5G NR Signals [with MATLAB Code]

The Low-altitude Economy Two-stage Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) Framework Based on 5G NR Signals
Abstract: The evolution of the next-generation wireless network has driven the thriving development of the low-altitude economy, supporting this emerging field while compatible with existing network architectures. Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) based on the new air interface (5G NR) signals is considered a highly promising solution. However, simply utilizing standard 5G NR signals such as Synchronization Signal Blocks (SSB) fundamentally limits the resolution in perception. To address this issue, this paper proposes a two-stage perception framework that starts from coarse to fine, combining the standard 5G NR initial access signal with a custom designed sparse pilot structure (SPS), achieving high-precision perception for drones. In the first stage, information from SSB, Type#0-Physical Downlink Control Channel (PDCCH), and System Information Block 1 (SIB1) is integrated to complete the initial detection of the target; in the second stage, an advanced estimation algorithm is introduced to break through the resolution limitations of the aforementioned signals. This phase is inspired by sparse array theory and designs a novel SPS, which is then inserted into resource blocks within the CORESET#0 bandwidth. To accurately extract distance and velocity parameters from these sparse pilots, this paper develops corresponding high-resolution algorithms based on Weighted Unwrapped Phase (WUP) technology and an iterative method based on RELAX. Finally, a Density-Based Spatial Clustering with Applications (DBSCAN) algorithm is employed to eliminate redundant detection results caused by beam overlap. Comprehensive simulation results show that compared to other technologies, the proposed framework achieves superior estimation accuracy and computational efficiency.
Introduction: The Evolution of Future Sixth Generation (6G) Wireless Networks Driven by Emerging Application Demands, with Low-Altitude Economy as a Frontier Field. As a new economic form, low-altitude economy utilizes the low-altitude airspace for various flight activities, expected to achieve revolutionary productivity enhancements in logistics distribution, environmental monitoring, and intelligent inspection. This large-scale economic activity is expected to involve millions of aircraft, hence the supporting network must possess dual capabilities: it should be capable of supporting massive real-time data communication and provide continuous, high-precision sensing capabilities to ensure safe and standardized control over the airspace.
To meet these integrated requirements, Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) has become a key enabling technology. By reusing 5G-A or future 6G communication signals, ISAC can simultaneously provide high-speed data transmission services for drones and achieve effective flight supervision. Relying on the seamless coverage of the communication network, it completes the perception, positioning, and monitoring of drones, thereby assisting in drone navigation, path planning, and airspace management. This is crucial for the development of low-altitude economies.
Existing research focuses on using ground base stations to support drone perception for low-altitude economies, with algorithms and frameworks proposed under both single-site and multi-site scenarios. However, these studies often do not consider the actual constraints of standards such as 5G NR and Wi-Fi, focusing more on theoretical aspects like beamforming and parameter extraction. To promote the practical deployment of ISAC, academia and industry have begun to focus on achieving perception within standardized protocol frameworks, with research primarily divided into two categories: data-based and pilot-based perception.
Based on data, a single-site perception relies on the prior knowledge of base station transmission data for drone parameter estimation. However, this method is entirely dependent on active downlink data transmission, which has randomness rather than continuity in perception capabilities, and there is a privacy risk of sensitive information leakage when using physical downlink shared channels (PDSCH) and other user-oriented data channels for perception. To escape from reliance on user data and address privacy issues, research has shifted towards dedicated reference signals independent of communication services. Positioning reference signals (PRS) and Channel State Information Reference Signals (CSI-RS) are potential choices, but these dedicated signals are typically transmitted only under specific network configurations and not continuously broadcasted in most operational networks, limiting their general application in continuous perception.
Synchronization signal blocks (SSBs) as core components of the 5G NR frame structure, with their periodic broadcasting characteristics, are ideal and highly reliable opportunities for perception. Perception based on SSBs has thus been widely studied. However, SSBs have inherent deficiencies: long transmission cycles limit the upper bound of unambiguous speeds, wide beamwidths and short single-beam orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) transmission times respectively restrict the accuracy of angle and velocity estimation. Existing studies attempt to alleviate radar ambiguity by combining SSBs with downlink control information (DCI) and SIB1, finding that the PDSCH carrying SIB1 only occupies a portion of the bandwidth allocated by the 5G NR standard, providing potential for inserting auxiliary pilots in idle resource blocks to enhance perception performance.
In the current research on assistive pilot design, uniformly spaced pilot patterns limit the unambiguous distance and speed. Subsequent studies have proposed designs such as coprime comb structures and irregular resource patterns, but these primarily focus on maximizing the unobstructed detection area without directly verifying the actual performance in distance and speed estimation; some also reframe the placement of pilots as a two-dimensional array design problem, reducing resource overhead, but rely on traditional two-dimensional fast Fourier Transform (2D-FFT), still constraining estimation accuracy. Inspired by these studies, this paper proposes a novel dual-stage estimation framework from coarse to fine, integrating 5G NR broadcast signals with a newly designed sparse pilot, achieving high-precision perception for drones while adapting to the constraints of the 5G NR protocol and breaking through the inherent limitations of standard signals.
Method Introduction: This paper aims to support low-altitude economics by designing a single-site MIMO-OFDM ISAC system that is compatible with the constraints of the 5G NR protocol. The base station is equipped with separate uniform linear array (ULA) transmit and receive antennas. Leveraging the beam scanning characteristics of the 5G NR broadcast signals, we propose a dual-stage drone perception framework from coarse to fine. In conjunction with sparse pilot design, high-resolution parameter estimation algorithms, and redundant detection pruning techniques, we break through the perception resolution limit of standard 5G NR signals. The core method is divided into two stages, which also include two key supporting technologies: sparse pilot structure design and DBSCAN redundant pruning. The overall process is as follows:
Phase 1: Robust initial detection and coarse parameter estimation of the target. This phase integrates information from three categories of 5G NR initial access signals: SSB, Type#0-PDCCH, and SIB1 to achieve robust initial detection of drone targets and coarse parameter estimation of angle, distance, and speed. The core steps include:
1  
Acoustic Analysis: The MUSIC algorithm is employed to estimate the direction of arrival (DoA) for targets within a single SSB beam. The number of potential targets within the beam is determined using the autocorrelation coefficient matrix method, followed by solving for target angles using the orthogonality between the array steering vector and the noise subspace.
2  
Space Filtering: Spatial filtering is applied to each estimated angle, suppressing interference and noise from other target clusters, thereby obtaining a simplified two-dimensional channel response model.
3  
Distance-velocity rough estimation: In response to the short SSB bandwidth and short observation time, this paper integrates the distance-Doppler (RDM) map of SSB and SIB1, as well as the high resolution distance profile of Type #0-PDCCH, to generate a joint distance-Doppler map. The target detection is completed by using the area-based fusion constant false alarm rate (ABC-CFAR) algorithm, obtaining the rough estimates of the target's distance, velocity, and angle.
Phase II: Parameter Estimation and Redundancy Detection Pruning
1  
Sparse Pilot Structure (SPS) Design: Influenced by the theory of sparse arrays, combined with the characteristic that SIB1 only occupies part of the bandwidth in CORESET#0 as specified by 3GPP standards, two types of sparse pilot structures, ANAII-2 and UF-4BL, were designed. They are adapted for CORESET#0 configurations of 48RB and 24RB, respectively, and are inserted into the idle resource blocks of SIB1. This design enhances the effective bandwidth of perception while reusing communication resources.
2  
Weighted Unwrapped Phase (WUP) + RELAX Iterations for Precise Estimation: First, implement single-target Doppler frequency (velocity) and delay (distance) estimation based on WUP technology to break through the fuzziness of traditional phase estimation. Then, combine the RELAX iteration's estimation-subtraction strategy with WUP to extend to multi-target scenarios, sequentially estimate each target's parameters and eliminate its interference, achieving high-precision extraction of multi-target distances and velocities. Finally, by pairing parameters, determine the distance-velocity parameter combination corresponding to each target.
3  
DBSCAN Redundant Pruning: To address the issue of multiple detections of a single target due to adjacent beam overlap in SSB/SIB1 beam scanning, the DBSCAN algorithm is employed in the joint domain of distance-velocity-angle for clustering. This process groups multiple detections of the same target into a single cluster, eliminates noise detection points, and weights the results within clusters for fusion, ultimately obtaining the final precise parameters of the target.
Furthermore, this paper derives the transmission and perception signal models for MIMO-OFDM ISAC systems, clarifies the transmission, echo, and sampling transformation processes of signals in both time and frequency domains, and establishes the Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) as a theoretical reference for parameter estimation performance.
Conclusion: The paper proposes a dual-stage ISAC framework based on 5G NR network, specifically designed to support the emerging low-altitude economic development. It effectively addresses the fundamental limitations of standard 5G NR broadcast signal perception resolution. The first stage of the framework integrates information from various 5G NR initial access signals such as SSB, Type#0-PDCCH, and SIB1. By generating a joint distance-Doppler map and combining with the ABC-CFAR algorithm, it achieves robust initial detection and coarse parameter estimation for unmanned aerial vehicle targets. The second stage, inspired by sparse array theory, inserts custom-designed sparse pilot structure (SPS) into the CORESET#0 bandwidth's idle resource blocks. Based on the weighted decoupling phase (WUP) estimator and RELAX iterative method, a high-resolution algorithm is developed to precisely extract the grid-distance and velocity parameters of targets in sparse pilots. At the same time, the DBSCAN clustering algorithm effectively eliminates redundant detection results caused by beam overlap.
Comprehensive simulation results validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework: compared to traditional benchmark methods such as 2D-FFT, 2D-MUSIC, and compression-sensing, this dual-stage framework not only achieves superior accuracy in distance and speed estimation but also exhibits superior performance in optimal sub-pattern allocation (OSPA) under multi-objective scenarios. It also boasts significantly higher computational efficiency. Moreover, different SPS configurations can be adapted separately for 24RB and 48RB CORESET#0 bandwidths, thereby optimizing perception performance. Future research will focus on two directions: first, verifying the perception performance of this framework in more complex dense multi-objective scenarios; second, further optimizing the design of sparse pilot patterns suitable for the 5G NR protocol to enhance its balance between perception performance and resource utilization.
